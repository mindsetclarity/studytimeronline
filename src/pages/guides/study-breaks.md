Breaks are part of studying, not time off from it. A good break lets you come back to the work with your attention restored. A bad break, usually one spent on a phone, leaves you more tired than before and makes the next block harder to start.

This guide covers how often to take breaks, how long they should be, and what to do with them.

## How often should you take a break?

A useful default is a short break after every 25 to 50 minutes of focused work, and a longer break after about two hours.

- **25 minutes on, 5 off** is the classic [Pomodoro](/guides/pomodoro-technique/) rhythm. It suits days when starting is hard, tasks you've been avoiding, and memorisation.
- **45 to 50 minutes on, 10 off** suits reading, problem sets and writing, where getting into the material takes a while and frequent stops would interrupt you.
- **A long break of 20 to 30 minutes** after three or four blocks gives you time to eat, go outside or deal with messages.

These are starting points, not rules. If you keep getting interrupted in the middle of a good stretch, lengthen the block. If your attention fades halfway through, shorten it. The [Pomodoro timer](/pomodoro/) lets you set both the focus and break lengths.

## Why scheduled breaks beat waiting until you're tired

Many students only stop when they feel exhausted. By then, the last 20 or 30 minutes were probably low quality: rereading the same paragraph, or staring at a problem without making progress.

Planning breaks in advance avoids this. You stop while you still have some energy, which makes it much easier to start again. It also takes away the constant question of "should I stop now?", which interrupts concentration every time you ask it.

## What to do in a short break (5 to 10 minutes)

The goal is to rest the specific kind of attention you were using, which usually means your eyes and your focus on a screen or page.

- **Stand up and move.** Walk to another room, stretch, or climb a flight of stairs. Moving helps you feel more alert when you sit back down.
- **Look at something far away.** After close reading or screen work, looking out of a window for a minute is restful for your eyes.
- **Drink water.** Mild dehydration makes concentration harder, and a trip to refill a glass is a natural break.
- **Get some daylight** if you can, even for a few minutes at a window or doorway.
- **Do nothing.** Sitting quietly and letting your mind wander is a real break. It's often when a problem you were stuck on suddenly makes sense.

## What not to do in a short break

**Scrolling social media.** It feels like rest, but it's another stream of information demanding your attention. It also has no natural end, so five minutes easily becomes twenty. If you want to check your phone, save it for a long break. See [how to stop checking your phone while studying](/guides/phone-distraction-while-studying/).

**Starting a new task.** Answering emails or tidying your desk "quickly" turns the break into more work and blurs where the study block ends.

**Heavy food.** A large meal in a short break can leave you sluggish. Keep proper meals for long breaks.

## What to do in a long break (20 to 30 minutes)

Long breaks are for the things short breaks can't fit:

- Eat a proper snack or meal.
- Go for a short walk outside.
- Reply to messages and check your phone, if you want to.
- Talk to someone.

Set a timer for the long break too. Without an end time, a long break can quietly become the rest of the afternoon.

## Getting back into work after a break

The hardest moment of any break is the end. Two habits make it easier:

1. **Before the break, write down the next step.** "Continue from question 6" or "finish the paragraph on causes". Then you come back to a clear instruction instead of having to work out where you were.
2. **Start the next timer right away.** Don't wait until you feel ready. Press start and do the next step for two minutes. Focus usually returns once you're moving.

## A sample study afternoon

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 14:00–14:45 | Focus block 1: reading with notes |
| 14:45–14:55 | Short break: water, stretch |
| 14:55–15:40 | Focus block 2: practice questions |
| 15:40–15:50 | Short break: walk around, look outside |
| 15:50–16:35 | Focus block 3: review mistakes |
| 16:35–17:05 | Long break: snack, messages, fresh air |
| 17:05–17:50 | Focus block 4: flashcards and a quick self-test |

That is three hours of real focus, which is a solid afternoon for most students. Use the [study planner](/study-planner/) to list the tasks for each block before you begin.
